# Shopping list

Hemmia turns recipes into a shopping list for the shops where you live: amounts in your units, the usual pack sizes, and the things you already have at home set aside. The family shares one list that everyone can add to and tick off, and everyone also has a list of their own. It's part of Hemmia at home.

<!-- steps -->
## Ask for a recipe
Ask Hemmia for a recipe, or for a few ideas for dinner. At the end of its answer Hemmia asks whether to put the ingredients on the shopping list. Say yes, and say for how many people if it's not the number in the recipe. They go on the family list; say "on my own list" if they're just for you.

## Or bring your own
Paste a recipe or a link to one and write "put this on my shopping list". Several recipes in one message work too; they end up on one list.

## Check what was added
Under the answer Hemmia shows how many items it added and any products it swapped for local ones. **Open list** shows the list, **Undo** takes it all off again, and **Move to my list** moves it to your own list if it shouldn't be on the family's.
<!-- /steps -->

## The list

Open **Shopping list** in the menu. Items are sorted by shop aisle. Tick an item when it's in your basket; bought items move down, and **Clear bought** removes them. Add anything yourself in the field at the top, with or without an amount: "milk", "2 l milk", "500 g minced beef".

The recipes on the list are shown at the top. Change the number of servings with **−** and **+** and the amounts follow; **×** takes a recipe off with its ingredients.

## When everything is ticked off

Once nothing is left to buy, the list is archived and starts empty. **Undo** brings it back if the last tick was a mistake. Finished lists stay under **Past lists** for 30 days: open one to see what was on it, or tap **Shop for these again** to put its items back on the list. After 30 days they're deleted.

## The family list and your own

At the top of the list you switch between **Family** and **Mine**; the number next to each says what is left to buy.

- **Family** is the list everyone at home sees: what someone adds shows up on the others' phones within a few seconds. Each item shows who added it, and bought items show who ticked them. Whoever goes shopping has everything on one list.
- **Mine** is only yours, for things the others shouldn't see yet, like a birthday present.

Open an item to move it to the other list. **Move to family list** at the top of your own list moves everything at once; items already on the family list are added up. **Clear list** on the family list clears it for everyone, so Hemmia asks first.

## How the amounts are worked out

Recipes measure for cooking, shops sell in packs. Hemmia adds up what all recipes on the list need and then rounds to what you can buy:

- Flour, sugar, butter, milk, cream and other staples: the smallest usual pack that is enough, for example 235 g flour becomes 1 kg.
- Meat and fish: close to the recipe, to the nearest 50 g.
- Vegetables and fruit: by the piece where that's how they're sold, otherwise by weight.
- Herbs by the bunch, garlic by the bulb, eggs by the box.

Tap an item to see where its amount comes from, for example "Pot pie: ⅔ cup heavy cream", and change it with **−** and **+**.

## Things you have at home

Salt, pepper, oil, flour, sugar and spices start under **At home already**, because most kitchens have them. Tap **Buy** if you've run out. For anything else, open the item and tap **I have it at home**, or tick **Always at home** so it's set aside next time too.

## Your country

Pack sizes and units depend on where you shop: kilograms and litres in Europe, pounds and quarts in the US, pints of milk in the UK. Hemmia picks the country of the computer it runs on; change it at the top of the list. Hemmia knows the usual sizes in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France, Italy, the UK and the US; elsewhere it uses metric units.

When a recipe needs something that isn't usually sold where you live, Hemmia suggests the local product and marks it on the list, for example "instead of heavy cream".

<!-- note -->
Recipes from the chat need a model that can use tools; most current models can. Your own list is stored on the hub, encrypted like your chats. The family list is encrypted with a family key kept on the hub, because everyone shares it. A list only reaches a model when you ask about it in a chat, and with a cloud model that is the provider you chose.
